Using Verlass

Verlass works much the same way in Gmail, in Outlook, and in the live demo, which is the quickest way to try anything described here. Where Gmail and Outlook differ, this page says so.

Sending and following a request

  1. Open Verlass beside your mail and select New request.
  2. Give it a short subject, choose the category that fits, and describe what happened and what you expected. If you are asked for a location, choose the closest one. Leave the priority on Standard for the category unless yours is different.
  3. Select Send. You get a number such as TKT-0042 and, unless your organization turned it off, a confirmation by email.
  4. Your requests are listed on the first screen, with unfinished ones first. Open one to read replies and to answer.
  5. If the problem goes away, select I no longer need this; anything you typed in the reply box goes along as a last message. If a finished request comes back, reply to it and it reopens.

Working the queues

People on the team list see the queues: Open (everything unfinished), Unassigned, Assigned to me, Overdue, Waiting, Finished, and All. Queues are in work order: overdue first, then the highest priority, then the soonest due time, then the oldest. Waiting tickets come after the ones being worked on.

  • Reply is saved on the ticket and emailed to the requester. Internal note is seen only by the team.
  • Changing the status can email the requester; each status decides that for itself. A status has a kind: open (being worked on), waiting (the ticket is not counted as overdue; its due time does not move), or done.
  • When a requester replies to a waiting ticket it returns to the first open status. When they reply to a finished ticket it reopens, if your settings allow.
  • Changing the priority sets a new due time from when the ticket was created, unless you set a due time yourself.
  • To log a request for someone who phoned or emailed, start a new request and fill in Requester email. With Create from the open email, Verlass fills in the subject, the message, and the sender for you.
  • If two people change the same ticket at once, the second is asked to open it again, so nobody's change is silently lost.

Settings

Administrators change everything from Settings, without editing code. In Gmail the same settings are also tabs in the help desk's Google Sheet; changes made in the Sheet show up within five minutes. When a value cannot be used, for example a color that is not a color, Verlass keeps a usable value and names the setting it corrected.

New help desks start from one of these presets: Office or business, School or college, Nonprofit or community group, Facilities and maintenance. A preset fills the categories and locations and sets the help desk's name and the location label; rename, remove, and add items freely. In Outlook and the browser app, an administrator can load another preset later.

Renaming. Tickets store the names of their status, priority, category, and location. To rename one, change its name and save: Verlass renames it on every ticket and rule that uses it and tells you how many tickets changed. A status keeps its kind when renamed, so a rename never reopens or finishes tickets. If you remove an item instead, its tickets keep the old name, and Settings lists them under "To review"; a ticket whose status is no longer on the list counts as open. Loading a preset renames nothing.

Renaming in Gmail. The Gmail list editor is plain text and cannot tell which line became which. Change only the name on that one line and save; if the same save also adds, removes, or moves lines, nothing is renamed.

In Gmail, lists are edited as text, one item per line with a vertical bar between the columns, and choices show their short names (for example business for working hours only).

How a new ticket is assigned

  1. The first assignment rule whose category, priority, location, and keyword all match. Empty columns match anything.
  2. Otherwise the person named on the ticket's category.
  3. Otherwise round robin among the active team, if that is turned on.
  4. Otherwise the ticket stays unassigned, and the whole team is told about it.

People marked inactive on the team list are skipped, so marking someone inactive while they are away sends their work to the next match.

Due times

Each priority allows a number of hours. With Working hours only, the clock runs only on your working days between the start and end times, and skips your days off. With the starting hours of 08:00 to 17:00, a ticket created on Friday evening with eight hours allowed is due at 16:00 on Monday. Set a priority's hours to 0 for no due time. The help desk's time zone is recorded when it is set up; check it under Hours.

Questions

Where is my data?

In Gmail, in one Google Sheet in the Drive of whoever set the help desk up. In Outlook, in three lists on a SharePoint site in your own Microsoft 365. Verlass has no server and its author never sees your tickets. See the privacy page.

Gmail: "You do not have access to the help desk's spreadsheet"

You are using a Standard install and the Sheet has not been shared with you. Ask your administrator to share it with you as Editor, or to move to the Private install, which needs no sharing.

Gmail: the add-on says it is not connected

In a Private install the add-on project needs a script property named SERVICE_URL holding the service's web app address, which ends in /exec. The first person to open the add-on after that must be the owner or an administrator.

Gmail: I changed something in the Sheet and the add-on does not show it

Settings are remembered for five minutes. Wait, or save any setting from the add-on, which refreshes them at once. Keep the tab names and the header rows as they are; Verlass finds settings by them.

Gmail: times look wrong

Set the Time zone setting, for example Europe/Berlin. Times in the Sheet itself are stored in universal time so they sort correctly.

Outlook: Microsoft says "Need admin approval"

Your administrator has not yet granted consent for the app registration. Send them the install guide.

Outlook: the panel shows "Set up the help desk"

The lists have not been created, or the add-in has not been given access to the site. If you are not an administrator, there is nothing for you to do; ask them to finish the install guide's steps "Create the lists" and "Set who can see what".

Outlook: "Your Microsoft 365 sign-in has ended"

Select Sign in again. In a browser tab, Microsoft's sign-in lasts about a day.

Outlook: a team member cannot see everyone's tickets

They need Full Control or Design on the Help Desk Tickets list (site Owners have it), and they must be on the Team list in Verlass's settings.

Can we use it for something other than IT?

Yes. Rename the help desk, change the word for a ticket, and replace the categories. The Facilities preset is one example, and the same works for HR questions, purchasing, or a front office.

How many tickets can it hold?

Verlass is built for small and medium organizations and has not been measured with large numbers of tickets. It reads the whole ticket list each time it draws a queue, so it slows down as the list grows. In Outlook, lists show at most 12,000 tickets. Archiving old rows or items keeps it quick.

How do we stop using Verlass?

In Gmail, uninstall the add-on and delete the Apps Script project or projects; the Google Sheet stays in Drive until you delete it. In Outlook, remove the add-in in the Microsoft 365 admin center, delete the app registration, and delete the three lists or the site. Nothing is stored anywhere else.

Something is not working

Please open an issue at github.com/alyssaagard/verlass/issues and say whether you use Gmail or Outlook, which install, and what you saw. Do not include private ticket details.
